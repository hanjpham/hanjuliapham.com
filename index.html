Research

Job Market Paper:

Can Funding Fix Rural Physician Shortages? Evidence from a Location Choice Model

Abstract: Physician geographic density affects access to care, especially in rural areas. This paper examines the location choice of early career physicians which includes standard demographics, practice environment proxies via Graduate Medical Education (GME) programs, and personal ties to the location. Using these demand parameters, I study the impacts of workforce policies by estimating the changes to rural probability when relevant attributes are varied in counterfactual analysis. I find that previous ties are major drivers of practice location: physicians have 49 times higher odds of choosing the location they did their residency in, but effects are halved in rural areas. Higher local hourly wages have little to no effects. Rural residency training exposure works through fostering previous ties to the location, underscoring the importance of local attributes in policy investments, and the potential of initiatives besides traditional approaches.
